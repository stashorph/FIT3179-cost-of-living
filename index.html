Essentials vs. Luxuries

Where the Australian Paycheque Really Goes

By Saiesh Jitendra Punganur • Monash University Data Visualisation • ABS & RBA Economic Series

Over the past decade, Australian households haven't been squeezed by extravagant lifestyle choices. From shelter and electricity to insurance and the weekly grocery run, unavoidable non-discretionary costs have dramatically outpaced discretionary spending—reshaping how families budget, who falls behind, and where Australians are moving.

The Big Squeeze: Needs Outrunning Wants

Headline inflation figures often obscure the daily reality of household budgets. By separating the Consumer Price Index (CPI) into Essentials (Non-Discretionary)—goods and services families cannot easily postpone, such as rent, electricity, medical care, and staple food—and Luxuries (Discretionary)—such as dining out, holidays, and consumer electronics—we reveal why living costs feel so punishing even when headline rates moderate.

A Decade of Two Regimes

Following the 2020 pandemic trough, supply disruptions and global stimulus ignited a sharp upward trajectory in consumer prices across 2022 and 2023. In response, the Reserve Bank delivered 13 successive rate rises, elevating borrowing costs to 12-year peaks.

Yet unlike discretionary inflation, which cooled rapidly as consumer sentiment weakened, essential living costs proved stubbornly sticky.

When Essentials Lead the Charge

The persistent divergence between essentials and luxuries illustrates a profound structural squeeze. Because household demand for shelter, groceries, and energy is price-inelastic, standard monetary tightening disproportionately penalises lower- and middle-income families who allocate the largest share of their earnings to basic survival.

Demographic Realignment

The Great Interstate Exodus

Over 140,000 Australians relocated interstate between 2020 and 2024. Queensland recorded the highest net intake in modern history, absorbing tens of thousands of families fleeing Sydney and Melbourne mortgage and rental burdens.

Unequal Price Shocks

Across the 11 major expenditure groups, volatility tells the true story of household instability. While energy and automotive fuel swung wildly due to geopolitical shocks, non-discretionary categories like food and rent rarely retreated, creating a ratchet effect that permanently lifted everyday expenses.

Key Takeaway

The Inelastic Dilemma

As monetary policy tightened, spending on dining and entertainment contracted, yet household utility bills and rents remained impervious. The decade from 2016 to 2026 demonstrates that true economic resilience requires stabilizing the fundamental baseline necessities of living.
