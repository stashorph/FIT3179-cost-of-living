FIT3179 DATA VISUALISATION 2 • AUSTRALIAN COST OF LIVING (2016–2026)

Essentials vs. Luxuries: Where the Australian Paycheque Really Goes

Over the past decade, Australian households haven't been squeezed by extravagant lifestyle choices. From shelter and electricity to insurance and the weekly grocery run, unavoidable non-discretionary costs have outpaced discretionary spending—reshaping how we spend, who falls behind, and even which states we move to.

SECTION 01

The Big Squeeze: Needs Outrunning Wants

Headline inflation tells only half the story. By separating the Consumer Price Index (CPI) into Essentials (Non-Discretionary)—goods and services households cannot easily avoid, such as rent, electricity, medical care, and staple food—and Luxuries (Discretionary)—like dining out, holidays, and electronics—we see why budgets feel tighter even as headline inflation moderates.

SECTION 02

Across Borders & Capitals: The Geography of Living Costs

Inflation does not hit Australia uniformly. State economies, local rental markets, and energy grids create distinct regional price pressures across our 8 States and Territories and 8 Greater Capital Cities—fueling a quiet exodus from Sydney and Melbourne toward Queensland and Western Australia.

Inside the Trolley: Category Winners & Losers

The ABS tracks 11 major CPI expenditure groups. Looking inside the basket reveals a rolling wave of inflation: first came automotive fuel and supply-chain goods, then fresh food and groceries, and finally sticky services like rents, insurance, electricity, and education.

SECTION 04

One Country, Five Realities: Who Hurts Most?

Why do working families often feel poorer than the official CPI suggests? Because the standard CPI excludes mortgage interest charges. The ABS Selected Living Cost Indexes (SLCI) measure out-of-pocket living expenses across five distinct Australian household demographics—revealing who truly carried the weight of the decade.
