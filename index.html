CHAPTER 01

The Big Squeeze: Needs Outrunning Wants

Headline inflation figures often obscure the daily reality of Australian household budgets. By separating the Consumer Price Index (CPI) into Essentials (Non-Discretionary)—goods and services households cannot easily avoid, such as rent, electricity, medical care, and staple food—and Luxuries (Discretionary)—like dining out, holidays, and electronics—we reveal the mechanics behind why living costs feel so unmanageable.

Macro Trends

A Decade of Two Inflation Regimes

Following the 2020 pandemic dip, global supply bottlenecks ignited a sharp upward trajectory in inflation throughout 2022–2023. In response, the Reserve Bank of Australia triggered 13 successive rate rises, bringing borrowing costs to multi-year peaks.

"Families cannot simply 'budget their way out' by skipping small luxuries—the baseline cost of existing has shifted permanently upward."

Core Insight

When Essentials Outpace Discretionary Items

The divergence between essential and luxury inflation highlights a structural shift. When essential items lead inflation, standard tightening mechanisms hit lower- and middle-income families hardest because essential demand is inelastic.
